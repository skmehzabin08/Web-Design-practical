Jawaharlal Nehru - Profile

Jawaharlal Nehru

First Prime Minister of India

Biography

Jawaharlal Nehru (1889–1964) was an Indian statesman, the leader of the Indian National Congress, and the first Prime Minister of independent India. He was one of the central figures in the Indian independence movement, working closely with Mahatma Gandhi to end British rule in India.

Born into an affluent family, Nehru was educated in prestigious institutions such as Harrow, Eton, and Cambridge, followed by law studies at London’s Inner Temple. His political career began in the early 1920s, during which he joined the Indian National Congress and quickly rose to prominence under the mentorship of Mahatma Gandhi. He became the country's first Prime Minister in 1947 when India gained independence.

Major Movements

  • Non-Cooperation Movement (1920-1922): Nehru played a significant role in this movement, where the Indian population was urged to boycott British goods and institutions.
  • Salt March (1930): Although led by Gandhi, Nehru was a prominent figure in the civil disobedience campaign against the British monopoly on salt.
  • Quit India Movement (1942): Nehru was a key leader during this mass protest calling for an immediate end to British rule in India.

Important Contributions

  • Instrumental in leading India to independence from British colonial rule.
  • Developed India's economy through industrialization, establishing large public sector enterprises.
  • Laid the foundation for modern science and technology education in India.
  • Promoted the policy of non-alignment during the Cold War.
  • Wrote "The Discovery of India", a seminal work on Indian history and culture.

Personal Life

Nehru was married to Kamala Nehru in 1916, and they had one daughter, Indira Gandhi, who would later become the Prime Minister of India. Nehru was known for his deep affection for children and his famous collection of letters written to them, called "Letters from a Father to His Daughter".
